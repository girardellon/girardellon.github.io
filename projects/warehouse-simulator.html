Problem Statement

Modern automated warehouses rely on multi-robot fleets to maximize throughput, presenting complex engineering challenges in path planning, coordination, and energy optimization. The objective was to determine how multiple autonomous robots could efficiently navigate a shared discrete 2D grid, fulfil dynamic orders, and avoid conflicts in real time without encountering computational bottlenecks.

Proposed Solution

I engineered a fully functional, object-oriented MATLAB application featuring a custom App Designer GUI. The core system implements grid-based path planning using both A* (Manhattan, Euclidean, and Chebyshev heuristics) and Dijkstra's algorithm. I developed a priority-based coordination framework for collision avoidance, a greedy nearest-robot algorithm for task assignment, and a dynamic battery consumption model that automatically routes depleted robots to charging stations.

Key Results

  1. Successfully simulated complex logistics scenarios involving up to six concurrent robots in dense, obstacle-rich environments.
  2. Demonstrated the computational efficiency of heuristic-guided A* compared with Dijkstra's algorithm, achieving identical path costs while exploring significantly fewer nodes.
  3. Validated the robustness of the priority-based collision avoidance and energy-aware task allocation mechanisms under intensive operating conditions.
