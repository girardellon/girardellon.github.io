Redundancy Resolution: Kinematic Control for Franka Emika Panda

Problem Statement

Redundant manipulators such as the 7-DOF Franka Emika Panda have more degrees of freedom than required for standard 6-D Cartesian tasks. The main engineering challenge was to accurately track a circular Cartesian trajectory while exploiting the robot's kinematic redundancy to avoid joint limits and reduce the risk of kinematic singularities.

Proposed Solution

I developed a Closed-Loop Inverse Kinematics (CLIK) control scheme based on the Jacobian pseudoinverse to execute the primary trajectory-tracking task. To exploit the additional degree of freedom, I implemented a null-space projection law (I − J+J) to incorporate secondary objectives—specifically, joint-centering gradient descent and Yoshikawa manipulability maximization—while preserving primary-task tracking. The control pipeline was validated in MATLAB and subsequently implemented as a C++ ROS 2 controller plugin for MuJoCo simulation.

Key Results

  1. Validated null-space projection, maintaining trajectory-tracking accuracy with and without secondary objectives enabled.
  2. Demonstrated effective joint-limit avoidance, reducing the average joint displacement index from 0.0627 to 0.0429, a reduction of approximately 31%.
  3. Developed a complete C++ ROS 2 control plugin integrating KDL-based forward kinematics and real-time Jacobian decomposition.
