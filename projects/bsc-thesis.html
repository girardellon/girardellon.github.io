Tactile-to-Visual Representation Learning for Vision-Language-Action Models

Problem Statement

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models generate robotic actions based primarily on visual input. During close-contact manipulation tasks, vision is frequently obstructed or degraded due to occlusions, lighting variations, or sensor failures. Retraining or fine-tuning a 7-billion-parameter VLA model on multimodal data is computationally expensive and risks compromising the semantic and visual knowledge acquired during pre-training.

Proposed Solution

Keeping the OpenVLA-OFT model weights completely frozen, I developed a lightweight projection module fθ to map embeddings generated by DIGIT optical tactile sensors directly into the policy's visual latent space (zV). This approach enables tactile signals to substitute for degraded visual input without modifying the downstream decision network. The simulation environment was developed by integrating TACTO tactile rendering into the MuJoCo/robosuite backend for the LIBERO manipulation benchmark.

Key Results & Contributions

  1. Representational Similarity Analysis (RSA): Demonstrated that a generic ViT-Small backbone achieves a higher cross-modal correlation with the visual latent space (ρ = 0.628) than the official TVL encoder (ρ = 0.372).
  2. Hardware Integration: Integrated TACTO tactile rendering into MuJoCo and developed the MJCF model of a Franka Panda gripper equipped with two DIGIT sensors.
  3. Pilot Evaluation: Built and validated a reproducible experimental pipeline, evaluating the projection network using leave-one-object-out cross-validation.
