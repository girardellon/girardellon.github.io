Tactile-to-Visual Representation Learning for Vision-Language-Action Models

Problem Statement

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models generate robotic actions based primarily on visual input. During close-contact manipulation tasks, vision is frequently obstructed or degraded due to occlusions, lighting variations, or sensor failures. Retraining or fine-tuning a 7-billion parameter VLA model on multimodal data is computationally expensive and risks compromising the semantic-visual knowledge learned during pre-training.

Proposed Solution

Keeping the VLA model (OpenVLA-OFT) weights completely frozen, a lightweight projection module fθ was developed to map embeddings generated by DIGIT optical tactile sensors directly into the visual latent space of the policy (zV). This approach allows the tactile signal to replace degraded vision without altering the downstream decision network. The simulation environment was built by integrating TACTO tactile rendering into the MuJoCo/robosuite backend on the LIBERO manipulation benchmark.

Key Results & Contributions

  1. Representational Similarity Analysis (RSA): Demonstrated that a generic ViT-Small backbone maintains a cross-modal correlation with the visual space (ρ = 0.628) significantly higher than the official TVL encoder (ρ = 0.372).
  2. Hardware Integration: Integrated TACTO rendering in MuJoCo, developing the MJCF model of a Franka Panda gripper equipped with two DIGIT sensors. Highlighted that the physical bulk of the sensors reduces the base success rate from 98.2% to 56.8% regardless of perceptual alignment.
  3. Pilot Evaluation: Built and validated a reproducible experimental infrastructure, evaluating the projection network via leave-one-object-out cross-validation.
